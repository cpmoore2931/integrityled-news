On December 4, 2024, Facilities Dive reported that the U.S. General Services Administration had awarded Constellation NewEnergy an energy savings performance contract worth nearly $120 million. The work covers five federally owned buildings in the Washington, D.C., area.

Facilities Dive, citing GSA, described those buildings as a 1950s federal courthouse and its annex, two 1960s buildings that house the Federal Aviation Administration, all in Washington, and a GSA building in College Park, Maryland. The scope is not lighting alone. GSA’s account of the project, as relayed in that report, says the updates will largely include LED lighting retrofits along with building controls, envelope work, utility distribution, water conservation, and heating and cooling plant electrification.

GSA expected the package to save $2.2 million a year in utility costs. That figure is GSA’s, as reported by Facilities Dive. This brief does not restate a bid tab or a lighting-only line item. The public write-up does not say how many luminaires, which product families, or which control system each building will use.

For contractors and suppliers watching federal work, the useful part is the shape of the job. Lighting is bundled inside a larger energy contract, not bid as a standalone lamp replacement. Submittals on that kind of project usually have to satisfy the energy service company’s performance guarantee, the building’s facility standards, and whatever the ordering office already keeps on its approved list. A fixture that is fine on a private warehouse can still be the wrong document set for a courthouse.

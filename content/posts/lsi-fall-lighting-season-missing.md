Inside Lighting’s November 9 write-up of LSI Industries’ investor call is useful because it names a pattern contractors already feel. The company’s usual late-summer and early-fall lighting push did not arrive.

LSI calls that stretch its lighting season. Building owners normally try to get exterior upgrades and maintenance done before the ground freezes and pole-base work gets hard. CEO James Clark told analysts the increase never came. He said some customers who had set capital aside at the start of the year moved the work into early next year. His line, as reported by Inside Lighting: “I don’t see any of the projects going away, but I do see an increased amount of customers kicking things down the road.”

The numbers behind that read are in the same report. Lighting sales were down 6 percent to $64.8 million. Lighting quotes over the most recent three months were up 5 percent from a year earlier. Orders were up 1 percent. CFO James Galeese said the pipeline is there and is backing up. When a job comes in over budget, customers go back and reengineer, which is value engineering by another name. The layout, the takeoff, and the price still had to be done, often by a rep who only gets paid when product ships.

Clark also gave a store-count picture that is not lighting-only. A customer plans ten renovations at $100,000 each. By late summer the same million dollars covers seven stores, and the other three slide. He put the cause on whole-build inflation, not on the price of any one product, and he said the disruption today is almost entirely budgeting. Inside Lighting flagged both lines as management’s estimate, not a measured industry statistic.

For a contractor, the practical comparison is local. If your fall exterior and maintenance work also slipped, you are not looking at a one-shop problem. If quotes are up and releases are flat, the unpaid hours are in the gap between the two. Clark’s bet is that the work comes back when calendars reset. The next two quarters of order data are the test.

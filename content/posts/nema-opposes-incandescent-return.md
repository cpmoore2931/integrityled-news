Facilities Dive reported that U.S. lighting manufacturers have no plans to restart production of general-use incandescent bulbs, even if Congress tries to reopen the market.

The National Electrical Manufacturers Association told the outlet that its members invested heavily in the switch to LEDs under efficiency standards that have been in place for years. Eric Bursch, NEMA’s director of government affairs, said the companies no longer make the older bulbs. A bill called the Liberating Incandescent Technology Act passed a Senate committee in a split vote in late September. It would reverse parts of a 2007 law that set minimum efficiency levels high enough that most common incandescents could no longer be sold.

NEMA and the American Lighting Association both oppose the bill. In a joint letter cited by Facilities Dive, the groups warned that many fixtures have been redesigned for the lower heat of LED lamps. Those fixtures are not rated for the higher heat of an incandescent bulb. Using one could create a fire risk.

The Department of Energy has said it supports giving consumers more choice, but the close committee vote makes a full Senate vote unlikely this year, according to NEMA. Foreign makers would be the ones best positioned to supply any returning demand.

For a contractor, the practical point is narrower than the politics. If a customer asks whether an older bulb will still fit a recent fixture, the answer is not automatic. Check the fixture rating. The manufacturers who built the current inventory say they are not going back.

This brief paraphrases the October 2026 Facilities Dive article. It does not predict whether the bill will pass.

Why do low LED bids get rejected on school jobs? In Aledo ISD's case, the engineer said the cheaper alternate fixtures did not show they met the energy code.

The Texas district's board agenda for November 16, 2026 included a vote on an LED lighting upgrade for five facilities: gyms at Aledo Middle School, Aledo High School and the Daniel Ninth Grade campus, plus an indoor practice facility. The district posted the packet on [BoardBook](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Public/Agenda/871?meeting=774413).

According to the district's board memo, three proposals came in on a designed package. The engineer, EMA Engineering & Consulting, recommended Hays Electric as the best overall value. The district's administration asked the board to approve $375,411 for Hays, paid from 2023 bond funds. That is the figure in the memo's recommendation line. EMA's October 8 letter lists the award amount as $375,114, and the memo's body has a third variant. The documents don't agree, so check the final contract for the real number.

The reason the lower-priced proposal lost is the useful part. EMA's letter says its team took a second look at the alternate lighting in that proposal. It found that the fixture configuration would draw more lighting power than the energy code allows. It also said there was not enough reliable photometric data to show the fixtures would hit the required light levels at compliant wattage.

The memo says procurement would go through a purchasing cooperative, with work starting at the Aledo Middle School gym as a proof of concept and done after hours. I could not confirm the board's vote from the sources I checked, so treat this as a recommendation, not a final award.

**Why it matters:** If you bid a public LED job with substitute fixtures, include photometric files and a wattage calculation against the energy code. A lower price gets thrown out when the engineer can't verify compliance.

Did Nashville finish its LED streetlight conversion? Yes. Nashville Electric Service (NES) says it has completed a project that upgraded more than 57,000 streetlights across the city, according to [WSMV News 4](https://www.wsmv.com/2026/11/23/nes-completes-project-upgrading-more-than-57k-streetlights-across-nashville/), which reported it on November 23.

NES CEO Teresa Broyles-Aplin said the program started a few years ago and finished about two years ahead of schedule. She put the savings at 2.4 million kilowatt-hours of energy a month, worth more than $200,000 a month. She also said maintenance volume is down 80% since the new lights went in. The utility says the fixtures also help crews spot outages faster.

Local outlet [Davidson County Source](https://davidsoncountysource.com/nashville-streetlight-project-finishes-early-1-5m-under-budget/) reported more detail in late October: the project began in early 2024 and was about $1.5 million under budget. It also reported $2.2 million in incentives from the Tennessee Valley Authority's EnergyRight program. Those extra figures come from that outlet, not from WSMV.

All of these numbers are reported by NES or the news outlets. I did not see an independent audit.

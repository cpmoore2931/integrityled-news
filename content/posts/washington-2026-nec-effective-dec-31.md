When does Washington switch to the 2026 NEC? December 31, 2026. The state Department of Labor & Industries (L&I) adopted its updated electrical rules on November 17, and says the 2026 NEC replaces the 2023 edition on that date.

The details come from L&I's [Concise Explanatory Statement](https://lni.wa.gov/rulemaking-activity/AO25-31/2531CES.pdf) for the rule change to Chapter 296-46B WAC. The comment period ran from August 5 to September 14, 2026, and L&I adopted the rule as proposed.

A few state changes that a lighting or electrical contractor might touch:

- **GFCI near showers.** Fixed electrical equipment with exposed grounded metal parts in areas subject to shower spray needs GFCI protection. L&I says this addresses a new bathroom design.
- **HVAC GFCI exception.** Listed HVAC equipment gets an exception to the new 2026 NEC GFCI requirements until June 30, 2028. An HVAC contractors' group asked L&I to keep it.
- **Energy storage.** A new section, WAC 296-46B-706, requires system design reviews for energy storage installations.
- **Plan review.** The electrical plan review rule now points to the 2026 NEC.
- **Renumbering.** Branch circuit, feeder and service calculations move to a new WAC 296-46B-120 to match NEC Article 120.

L&I also fixed one typo in the adopted text: a reference to "NEC 2130.70(B)" is now 230.70(B).

I did not check how local jurisdictions in Washington handle permits already in the pipeline. Ask your inspector.

**Why it matters:** If you pull permits or bid Washington work that will be installed after Dec. 31, 2026, price and draw it to the 2026 NEC and the state amendments, not the 2023 code.

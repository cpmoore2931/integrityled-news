Hoodline reported that Germantown, Tennessee, approved a contract to convert every one of its 5,346 street lamps to LED. The board vote was Monday. The total is nearly $3 million, including a $150,000 contingency. Work is expected to start before the first of the year and take about six months.

The contractor is PATH, the same company that has handled similar conversions in nearby Collierville, Bartlett, and several smaller towns, according to the Daily Memphian reporting cited in the Hoodline story. Not every suburb used the same path. Arlington and Millington worked directly with Memphis Light, Gas and Water instead.

The numbers are specific enough to be useful. A mid-size suburb is treating a full streetlight swap as a capital project with a clear start window and a six-month finish. Nearby Memphis has already converted a large share of its own inventory. The pattern is local, not national, but it shows the work is still being bid and awarded.

For a contractor who bids municipal lighting, the useful detail is the scope: one city, every lamp, one contractor, a fixed timeline. The public write-up does not list fixture models or warranty terms. Those stay on the bid documents.

This brief summarizes the November 10, 2026 Hoodline article and the Daily Memphian reporting it cites. It does not claim a national trend beyond the Memphis-area examples given.

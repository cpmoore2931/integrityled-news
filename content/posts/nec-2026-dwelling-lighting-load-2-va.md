Did the 2026 NEC change the lighting load for homes? Yes, for one number. The general lighting load used for dwelling feeders and services drops from 3 VA per square foot to 2 VA per square foot.

That comes from a November 13, 2026 column by Mark C. Ode in [Electrical Contractor Magazine](https://www.ecmag.com/magazine/articles/article-detail/doing-the-math--calculating-lighting-factors--part-2). He points to NEC 120.41 for the new 2 VA/ft² feeder and service value. He says it covers one-family, two-family and multifamily dwellings. His reason: LEDs have replaced incandescent lighting, so the load is lower.

**What did not change.** The branch-circuit number is still 3 VA per square foot under NEC 120.13. Ode warns that many contractors and engineers will miss the split. EC&M made the same point in a [July 2026 explainer](https://www.ecmweb.com/national-electrical-code/video/55389691/2026-nec-changes-damaged-wiring-conductor-replacements-10a-branch-circuits-and-dwelling-general-lighting-loads): don't use the lower value to shrink branch-circuit capacity.

**His example** follows the 2026 NEC Annex D1(a) single-family house, 1,500 square feet.

- Feeder and service lighting: 1,500 ft² × 2 VA = 3,000 VA.
- Branch-circuit lighting: 1,500 ft² × 3 VA = 4,500 VA. At 120V that is 37.5A, so three 15A circuits or two 20A circuits.

Ode also says floor area follows NEC 120.5(C). Open porches and detached garages are left out. A basement or cellar that could be finished into living space may need to be counted.

**Check your edition first.** This only applies where the 2026 NEC is adopted. Many places still enforce older editions. See [the NEC edition is a local question](/news/nec-edition-is-a-local-question), and more in [code updates](/news/category/code-updates).

This brief summarizes the published column. It is not a code interpretation. Check the adopted text and your inspector.

**Why it matters:** On a dwelling bid or permit under the 2026 NEC, size the feeder and service at 2 VA/ft² but keep branch circuits at 3 VA/ft². Mixing them up gives you a wrong load calc.

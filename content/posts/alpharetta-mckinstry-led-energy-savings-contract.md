Yes, Alpharetta, Georgia just bought a big LED retrofit. On November 16, 2026, the City Council approved a $3,340,105 energy savings contract with McKinstry. [Alpharetta Now](https://alpharettanow.com/alpharetta-energy-contract-vote-city-buildings-nov-16/) reported the vote was unanimous. The city's own meeting agenda lists the item as an Energy Savings Guarantee Contract to McKinstry Inc., plus lease-purchase financing through Bank of America.

Lighting is the biggest line a contractor will care about. Per the outlet's reading of the staff report, the work replaces 3,750 fixtures with LED at 43 sites. The package also covers 109 networked thermostats, seven HVAC units, building sealing and window tint, and low-flow water fixtures.

Here is how Alpharetta Now says it is paid for:

- $1,950,000 from a Bank of America loan, 15 years at a fixed 4.62%
- $1,207,302 from the city's 2027 budget
- $182,803 in utility incentives and rebates

The outlet also reports McKinstry guarantees $176,243 in savings in year one, rising about 3% a year for 20 years. If savings come up short, McKinstry owes the city the difference. Those are guarantees from the staff report, not measured results. Nothing is installed yet.

One more detail from the staff report, as the outlet describes it: city crews have replaced 133 fixtures since October 2025, at about two hours each. Some fixtures at City Hall need a motorized lift. That is the reason given for doing the lighting in one bulk job.

This brief paraphrases the Alpharetta Now article, which was updated November 17. We did not review the full contract.

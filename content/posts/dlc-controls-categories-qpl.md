EC&M published a write-up by a DesignLights Consortium technical manager on the new Controls Categories that SSL V6.0 added to the Qualified Products List. The change is meant to stop the guesswork that has slowed rebate-eligible controls work.

Before, if any version of a product had an integral control, the whole family could show as “yes” on the list. Specifiers still had to open the cut sheet to see which model number actually included occupancy or daylight harvesting. Under V6.0, manufacturers submit a controls options table. Each model maps to a standardized Controls Category. A new Model Number Search shows the category and a confidence percentage for the match.

The article notes that the same transition still has a hard date. Products that missed the October 9 update window can come off the list on December 15. Some programs have said they will offer a grace period. Others may not. The advice is the same as on the earlier V6.0 briefs: check the live listing for the exact catalog number before you lock a quote that runs past that date.

DLC research cited in the piece puts average savings from networked controls at about 49 percent beyond standalone LEDs, higher in some building types. That figure is from DLC’s own reporting, not a count we ran. The practical takeaway for a contractor is narrower. The list is now clearer about which controls a specific model actually has. Use the search, not last year’s PDF.

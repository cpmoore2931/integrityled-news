Which NEC edition applies in Fort Smith, Arkansas? The 2026 NEC with Arkansas amendments, starting December 1, 2026. The city says it adopted the code on November 17.

The city's [bulletin](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/ARFORTSMITH/bulletins/43340c5) says the change covers electrical contractors, general contractors, designers, developers and homeowners. It says the move lines the city up with the state's electrical standards.

Van Buren followed on Monday, November 23. The [River Valley Democrat-Gazette](https://www.rivervalleydemocratgazette.com/news/2026/nov/24/van-buren-city-council-approves-city-electrical/) reports the City Council voted unanimously for an emergency ordinance adopting the 2026 NEC with the state's amendments. The paper says the city had to comply with the new state code within 90 days.

A few local details from the Van Buren story:

- Extra trip or temporary service inspection fees go to $50, up from $40.
- There are new rules on island and peninsula receptacles.
- A city building official described a rule that a home remodel touching at least 50% of the electrical system means bringing the whole system up to the latest code. A council member questioned it, so confirm the final wording with the city.

The paper quotes the city's planning director saying the Arkansas Board of Electrical Examiners sets the state standard, and that all work is judged and inspected against it.

I did not check the Arkansas amendment text itself, so read it before you price anything. Other states are moving too. See our brief on [Washington's Dec. 31 switch](/news/washington-2026-nec-effective-dec-31) and more in [code updates](/news/category/code-updates).

**Why it matters:** If you pull permits in Fort Smith or Van Buren, design and price to the 2026 NEC with Arkansas amendments, and ask the building office which edition applies to work already in the pipeline.

Does a lock with a label on it count as the lock and tag under OSHA's electrical work rule? Yes, according to a letter OSHA issued on August 17, 2026.

The question came from a stakeholder about 29 CFR 1910.333(b)(2)(iii). That section says a lock and a tag go on each disconnecting means used to de-energize equipment. It also says the tag must carry a statement prohibiting unauthorized operation of the disconnect and removal of the tag.

The person asked whether a lock with a permanently attached label would do the job. The label read: "unauthorized operation of the disconnecting means and unauthorized removal of the lock are prohibited."

OSHA's [letter of interpretation](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/standardinterpretations/2026-08-17-0) says yes. It points out that 1910.333 doesn't set a format for the tag. That matches the broader lockout/tagout standard, 1910.147, which describes a tagout device as a prominent warning device that can be securely fastened. It doesn't have to take any particular form. The letter was signed by Erin P. Gilmore, acting director of OSHA's Directorate of Enforcement Programs. [Safety+Health Magazine](https://www.safetyandhealthmagazine.com/osha-letter-of-interpretation-looks-at-lockout-tagout-labels/) reported on it on November 10.

Keep in mind what this is. OSHA says its interpretation letters explain existing rules and don't create new employer obligations, and this one answers only the question that was asked. It covers a lock with that exact kind of label, not every lockout setup.

**Why it matters:** If your crew uses labeled locks for electrical lockout, cite this letter in your safety plan or bid submittal. Make sure the label is permanent, easy to read, and says what OSHA quoted.
